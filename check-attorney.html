What does this mean?

Dismissal rate is the percentage of an attorney's Chapter 13 cases that were dismissed (failed) rather than discharged (succeeded). A dismissed case means the debtor loses their filing fee, usually doesn't get attorney fees back, and is back where they started.

The national average Chapter 13 dismissal rate is 26.1%, computed from 20.4 million cases across all 94 federal bankruptcy districts. Some case failure is normal. But when an attorney's rate is significantly above the district average, it suggests a pattern.

District baseline is the average dismissal rate for all attorneys in the same court. This controls for local factors (judge behavior, debtor demographics, economic conditions). An attorney above their own district's baseline is underperforming relative to their peers in the same courtroom.

This data comes from the FJC Integrated Database, CourtListener RECAP, and public PACER records. No private data is used.
