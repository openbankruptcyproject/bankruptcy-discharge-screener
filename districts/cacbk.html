Bankruptcy in the Central District of California

Quick answer. Between 1977 and 2026, the searchable federal record shows 278,114 bankruptcy cases filed in the Central District of California (the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Central District of California). Where the filing chapter is recorded, the most common is Chapter 7. This page summarizes what the public data shows about filing here - the court, the chapters, the judges, and how the district compares to national outcomes. The searchable record is a partial index of historical filings -- coverage is much deeper for recent decades than early ones -- so the count above reflects what is indexed, not the court's lifetime filing volume.

The court

The Central District of California is served by the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Central District of California. The court hears cases across five divisions, with courthouses in Los Angeles, Santa Ana, Riverside, Woodland Hills (San Fernando Valley Division), and Santa Barbara (Northern Division). It is frequently described as the largest bankruptcy court in the United States by filing volume.

How this district compares

Nationally, the FJC Integrated Database of about 8 million bankruptcy terminations analyzed for 2008-2024 shows an overall discharge rate of 58.3% and a dismissal rate of 41.7% (of the 8M analyzed terminations). Outcomes vary sharply by representation: pro-se Chapter 13 debtors reach discharge about 2.9% of the time versus roughly 47% for represented Chapter 13 debtors (FJC 2008-2024).

Frequently asked questions

Which court handles bankruptcy in the Central District of California? The United States Bankruptcy Court for the Central District of California handles federal bankruptcy filings for the district. The court hears cases across five divisions, with courthouses in Los Angeles, Santa Ana, Riverside, Woodland Hills (San Fernando Valley Division), and Santa Barbara (Northern Division). It is frequently described as the largest bankruptcy court in the United States by filing volume.

What chapters do people file here? Across the searchable record, filings appear under Chapters 7, 11, 12, and 13. Where a chapter is recorded, Chapter 7 is the most common in the Central District of California. Chapter 7 (liquidation) and Chapter 13 (individual repayment plan) are the most common nationwide; Chapter 11 (reorganization) is used mainly by businesses and higher-debt individuals; Chapter 12 is for family farmers and fishermen.

How does the Central District of California compare to the rest of the country? National outcome rates come from the FJC dataset described above (8M terminations, 2008-2024). This page reports the district's own case counts from the searchable federal record; a district-specific FJC outcome rate is not shown here.

Data and methodology

  • Case counts are of the searchable federal record (a national index of about 9.8 million

cases), filtered to the Central District of California (source district code cacbk). This is a count of records in that index, not an official court statistic.

  • Chapter is recorded for 2.05% of these cases (5,705 of 278,114); per-chapter

counts are floors, and the recorded-chapter subset reflects docket-retrieval coverage rather than a random sample of filings. An additional 54 records are adversary-proceeding entries with a recorded "AP" code; they are included in the total but excluded from the chapter table.

  • Judges and Chapter 11 status come from an enriched dataset of Chapter 11 cases; that dataset

has not yet reached this district (data pending; enrichment queued 2026-07-04).

  • National rates (58.3% / 41.7%, and the pro-se gap) are from the **FJC Integrated Database,

~8 million terminations analyzed 2008-2024** - the baseline for all rates on this page. The 9.8M figure is the size of the searchable index only and is never used to compute a rate.

  • Names: individual debtor names are reduced to initials; business names are kept. This page

reports aggregates only.
